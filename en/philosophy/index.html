Our investment philosophy

Price beforeforecast.

We do not try to predict which economic narrative will prove correct. We build an investment approach that responds to what is actually happening in the market.

We do not predict the market. We follow it.Objective rules, broad diversification and discipline keep the process consistent even as market conditions change.
The foundation of trend following

Markets move in trends.

Financial markets often develop extended price movements as economic, political and psychological forces affect supply and demand over time.

This is particularly visible in commodity markets. High prices stimulate production and restrain demand. Over time, a surplus can push prices lower. Low prices can in turn reduce production and investment, tighten supply and lay the foundation for the next upswing.

Such processes unfold gradually and may be reinforced by policy, credit conditions, geopolitics, weather, technological change and market psychology. The result can be prolonged rises or declines. Trend following seeks to identify these movements and participate for as long as they persist.

EconomyInflation, interest rates and currency cycles
SupplyCommodity shortages, overproduction and weather
ChangeGeopolitics, technology and investor behaviour
Rules-based process

Trend before opinion.

The investment process is based on predefined rules rather than subjective market assessments. Clear criteria for signals, exposure and exits make the process consistent and suitable for analysis across different market environments.

  1. Follow clear movements

    Rising markets can be bought and falling markets can be sold. When direction is absent, exposure is reduced or avoided.

  2. Adjust exposure

    Stronger trends may receive greater weight than weaker movements, while exposure is reduced when volatility rises.

  3. Manage risk

    Position sizing and exits are governed by predefined rules.

  4. Let the trend continue

    Positions that develop positively are given room for as long as the trend persists. The process requires discipline and patience.

When trends are absent

Sideways and erratic markets can lead to weaker results. Periods without a clear direction are a natural part of the return profile of trend following.

Risk management in practice

  • Controlled exposure in individual positions
  • Lower exposure when volatility rises
  • Diversification across markets and asset classes
  • Continuous monitoring of the portfolio’s total exposure

Absolute return and diversification.

Trend-following strategies can take both long and short positions and are therefore not mechanically dependent on rising equity prices.

Trading takes place across markets with different drivers, including commodities, equity indices, interest rates, currencies and other global assets. The purpose of trading markets with different drivers is to reduce dependence on any single market.

Complement

Quantitative macro trading.

Quantitative macro models can complement trend following by operating over shorter time horizons and using different market relationships.

01

Data-driven analysis

Historical market data are analysed to identify recurring patterns, statistical deviations, changes in volatility and behaviour across different time horizons.

02

Systematic execution

Signals, order placement, position sizing and exit rules are governed by predefined rules. The human role lies primarily in research, development and oversight.

03

Continuous development

Short-term market effects can change. New ideas are tested and evaluated, while models that lose effectiveness are gradually phased out.

04

Diversification across models

Strategies with different signals and time horizons are combined in an effort to reduce dependence on a single model or market environment.

A robust investment approach does not depend on a single forecast, market or model.

Risk control and adaptation are central parts of the process.
